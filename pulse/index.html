Privacy Policy — Pulse (Private Journal)

Application ID: com.nabd.journal · Android

Effective date: 29 September 2026

100% offline · No account · No tracking

Pulse is a private journal that stores everything on your own device. This policy explains exactly what happens to your information when you use the app.

1. The short version

Your journal entries, moods, tags, photos and audio recordings never leave your phone. There is no account to create, no email address to give, and no server that receives your writing. The app contains no analytics software, no crash-reporting service, and no third-party tracking.

2. What stays on your device

Everything you create is written to your device's private storage:

  • Journal entries (text, titles, tags, pinned and favourite flags)
  • Mood records and your mood history
  • Photos you attach to entries
  • Audio recordings you make for an entry
  • Your seed garden, achievements, streaks and challenges
  • Tags, search preferences and app settings

All of this is stored in an encrypted database. Pulse uses AES-256-GCM encryption for data at rest; the encryption key is generated on your device and held in the operating system's secure keystore. Without your device passcode or biometric, the journal content cannot be read.

3. What leaves your device, and why

DataPurposeWho receives it
Advertising identifierShowing advertisements from Google AdMobGoogle

These are the only two cases in which any identifier is transmitted. No journal content, mood data, photo, audio file, or personal detail is ever included in a request.

Advertising

The free version of Pulse displays advertisements provided by Google AdMob. Google may use the device's advertising identifier to serve and measure ads. Pulse shows a rewarded advertisement only when you choose to watch one in exchange for a reward. Removing ads is part of the paid tier. For details on Google's use of the advertising identifier, see Google Ads policies.

In-app purchases

Purchases are processed entirely by Google Play. Pulse receives only a confirmation that a purchase succeeded. Payment card details are never seen by this app and never stored on your device.

4. What we do NOT collect

We do not collect:

  • Your name, email address, phone number or any contact details
  • Any account or login information — the app has no sign-in
  • Your location or GPS coordinates
  • Your contacts, call log, or SMS messages
  • Your browsing history or installed apps
  • Usage analytics or profiling data
  • Your journal text, mood records, photos, or audio

5. On-device text analysis

Pulse offers features that analyse your writing — for example, suggesting a mood, proposing tags, or summarising an entry. This analysis runs entirely on your device using built-in rules and dictionaries. It is not artificial intelligence hosted in the cloud, and your text is not transmitted to any external service to be analysed.

Every permission is optional. If you decline one, the corresponding feature is unavailable and the rest of the app continues to work.

7. Your control over your data

  • Delete everything. Settings offers a control that erases all journal content, mood history, photos, audio and settings from your device in a single action.
  • Encrypted backup. You can export an encrypted, password-protected backup file and re-import it later. You choose where that file is stored; we never receive it.
  • App lock. You can require a fingerprint, face, or passcode every time the app opens.

8. Children

Pulse is not directed at children under 13. Because the app requires no account and collects no personal information from anyone, it does not knowingly gather data from children.

9. Your rights

Under the GDPR, CCPA and similar laws you have the right to access, correct, export, or delete your personal data. Because all data is stored only on your device and is never transmitted to us, you can exercise every one of these rights directly from within the app without contacting us.

If this policy changes, the updated version will be published at this URL with a new effective date and noted in the app's release notes.
