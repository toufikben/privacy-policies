Deleting your Pulse data

Pulse: Private Journal (com.nabd.journal) · by Toufik Bendjeddah · Updated 15 October 2026

Pulse keeps everything you write on your own phone. That means most of your data is deleted by you, in the app, in one action — no request to us needed. Two things are covered below: the journal data on your device, and the small purchase record created when you buy Pulse Pro.

1. Delete your journal data yourself (instant)

Open Pulse → Settings → Data → Delete All Data and confirm the dialog. This erases from your device, in one action: every journal entry; your mood history, tags, seed garden and other saved content; the photos and audio recordings stored with them; your themes, language and other settings; the encryption key and passcode, plus the app lock; and any reminder schedules. A fresh encryption key is created for the restarted app. It cannot be undone. Nothing needs to be sent to us, because none of this data was ever transmitted to us.

You can also simply uninstall the app; Android removes its private storage with it. Clearing the app's storage from Android Settings → Apps → Pulse → Storage → Clear data has the same effect while keeping the app installed.

The in-app control does not remove the small purchase record described in section 2 — that record is not on your phone, so only we can delete it.

2. Delete your Pulse Pro purchase record (ask us)

When you buy or restore Pulse Pro, the app sends Google Play's purchase token, the product ID and a random identifier generated on your phone to RevenueCat, which checks the purchase with Google. That record contains no journal content, no name, no email address and no phone number.

To have that record removed, email toufik.bendjeddah@gmail.com with the subject line Pulse data deletion. Tell us roughly when you bought Pulse Pro and which device you used. We delete the corresponding subscriber record in RevenueCat, usually within a few days and always within 30 days, and confirm to you when it is done.

Deleting the purchase record means Pulse can no longer verify your Pulse Pro entitlement on a new install. You can buy again, or restore the purchase from the app if the record still exists in Google Play.

The copy of the random identifier kept on your phone is private app storage: uninstalling Pulse, or clearing its data in Android Settings, removes it.

3. What we cannot delete for you

  • Backups you made yourself. If you exported an encrypted backup file, it lives wherever you saved it; delete that file yourself.
  • Google Play's own purchase records. Order history held by Google is covered by Google's privacy policy and must be requested through Google Play support.
  • Dictation audio handled by your phone. Pulse does not store or forward it; the speech service on your device decides its own retention.

4. Your other rights

Under GDPR, CCPA and similar laws you can also ask us about access, correction or export of any personal data we hold. The same email address applies. Because Pulse has no accounts and no journal server, there is no personal dataset for us to export on your behalf — the app's own export tool produces the encrypted backup file described above.

Full details are in the Pulse Privacy Policy.
